Landing page (PGHL_LANDING=1)

The project’s public start page for gohyperlevel.com, served by the app itself – server-rendered, no cookies, no tracking, nothing loaded from third parties. Off by default: without the flag none of its routes exist and / is the web app (logged-out visitors land on the sign-in page at /app).

Path Page
/ · /de the landing page, English (primary, for X, Reddit and Hacker News links) and German; /en redirects to /
/vergleich · /de/vergleich the full feature × vendor comparison
/vergleich/highlevel · /de/vergleich/highlevel for people switching from HighLevel: price facts, what we offer instead, the importer
/sitemap.xml the six pages (plus /cloud with cloud mode); /robots.txt names it

Only on the instance’s own host. A request for any other host – a client’s white-label login domain, a cloud sub-domain – is sent to /app, so no client ever sees the project’s marketing page. Website and help-centre domains are rewritten to their site before routing and are not affected.

The product name comes from Agentur → Branding; the shipped default (“HyperLevel”) shows as HyperLevel. There is no separate brand variable. The mark (web/public/icon.svg) stands wherever the brand shows: header, footer, the app download buttons, the favicon (/icon.svg, /favicon.ico, apple-touch-icon.png) and the social cards – never a bare letter.

What it shows

Section titled “What it shows”

The home page leads with the agentic side, in this order:

  1. Hero – “Your platform for” / “Deine Plattform für” with rotating words in gold (AI agents, sales & CRM, funnels & websites, email marketing, online courses, bookings, invoicing – pure CSS, one word at a time, the first one only with prefers-reduced-motion; every language needs the same number of words), who it is for as a smaller line below (“for agentic solopreneurs, agencies, small businesses & startups” / “für agentische Solopreneure, Agenturen, kleine Unternehmen und Startups”; screen readers get the whole sentence from the heading’s label), a sub line on Jarvis and “one single source of truth”, the three calls to action (live demo, self-host in one command, cloud), the GitHub link with the star count, a badge row (MCP, your own Claude/ChatGPT plan for your own chats …) and a crossfade of up to four real screens – shot-jarvis.webp first when it exists, then pipeline, inbox, workflow editor, website builder (still with prefers-reduced-motion).
  2. Integrates with your favorite tools – an endless row of integration marks right under the hero (server/services/landing-logos.ts): only integrations that exist, each with where it lives (via: a key of the integrations catalogue or a file and a word in it – tests/landing.test.ts checks every one against the running app). No vendor the page compares against (competitors.json, replaces.json) and no AI lab’s mark – Claude, OpenAI and Gemini are plain words; Zoom, DATEV, Outlook, Microsoft Teams, Mollie and Norman too. Marks from simple-icons 13.21.0 (CC0), inline SVG in one hidden sprite, monochrome in the hero’s muted ink, the brand colour on the icon on hover (where it keeps 3:1 on the ink). CSS only: the list twice in a row slides by its own width, hover or keyboard focus (the row is a focusable region) pauses it, edges fade out, prefers-reduced-motion shows a still, wrapped grid; the copy of the list is aria-hidden. Under it: “Logos are trademarks of their owners and show available integrations.”

A small HTML diagram, no picture.
  5. Jarvis (#jarvis) – the voice companion and business developer of focus mode (living-boards.md): growth briefing with top‑3 opportunities and risks in € (opt-in), weekly review, check-ins and event alerts, drafts that wait for a click (voice never confirms), conversation mode, the fallback chain. Cloud waitlist mode

Section titled “Cloud waitlist mode”

While the hosted cloud is not open – cloud mode without a Stripe key, or PGHL_CLOUD_WAITLIST=1 (cloud.md → Waitlist mode) – the page sells nothing that cannot be bought:

  • Self-hosting first. The hero’s main button is Kostenlos selbst hosten / Self-host for free (to the install section), then the live demo, then Cloud: auf die Warteliste / Cloud: join the waitlist. Eyebrow, sub line, badges (“Dein Server, deine Daten” instead of “Gehostet in Frankfurt”), meta description and Open Graph texts say the same.
  • Plans stay, marked as planned. The cloud cards carry Bald verfügbar and voraussichtlich before the price; their button Auf die Warteliste / Join the waitlist opens the page with the plan preselected in the form (?plan=team#warteliste). The self-hosted card has the primary button. No trial, card, Stripe Tax or “start now” wording anywhere.
  • The waitlist form (#warteliste, below the prices): name (optional), e-mail, plan, a consent checkbox with the privacy link, a honeypot. It is a plain form post – no script, the CSP’s form-action 'self' covers it – to POST /api/public/cloud-waitlist, which sends the visitor back with ?wl=ok and the thank-you note (“Danke! Wir melden uns, sobald die Cloud startet.” / “Thanks! We’ll be in touch as soon as the cloud launches.”), or with the error above the form. Pages with ?wl= are served no-store. With PGHL_LANDING_LOCATION the form shows the HQ form’s own consent text and requires the first name (company-playbook.md).
  • Everything else that spoke of the cloud as available is reworded (server/services/landing-waitlist-copy.ts): Cloud oder selbst hosten? puts self-hosting first and marks the cloud column planned, the calculator says “Cloud (geplant)”, the stack total “Cloud geplant”, the HighLevel table “Cloud Solo geplant: 49,99 €”, the FAQ answers (plus “Kann ich die Cloud schon nutzen?”), the white-label lead, the Android and iPhone notes, and the phrases of the Warum wechseln? paragraphs that quote cloud prices or Frankfurt hosting. Research notes about our cloud in the comparison matrix and the stack table get “(Cloud: geplant, noch nicht verfügbar)”; the matrix price row says “0 € · Cloud geplant”.

Social shares get og-waitlist-<lang>.png instead of the card with “Cloud ab 49,99 €”. There is no JSON-LD on the page, so there are no offers to change.

Rules the copy follows: factual comparative advertising (no logos of other vendors, no claims that cannot be shown, every price with source and date, the disclaimer under every comparison and in the footer: HyperLevel is an independent open-source project and is not affiliated with HighLevel Inc. or the other vendors named). Gaps are said plainly: SaaS mode is part of self-hosting, not of the cloud; our branded app is an installable web app plus a self-built Android APK, not a store app; the HighLevel importer moves contacts, custom fields, tags, pipelines, deals, calendars and appointments, not funnels or workflows. Testimonials are left out until real quotes exist. Your own ChatGPT or Claude subscription is always “for your own chats” (automations use API keys), XR always “beta”, Jarvis’s growth briefing always “you switch it on”. “Built with Claude” appears as plain words in the footer, never as a logo.

The stack research

web/public/landing/replaces.json:

coverage is full, partial (the note says what is missing) or unique (what only we offer – for the MCP row: self-hostable). A row with "in_total": false is shown with an asterisk but not counted. Images

Section titled “Images”

npm run landing:assets (after npm run build) seeds a fictional demo, sets the product name to HyperLevel, takes the four hero screens at 1440×900 (shot-workflow|inbox|funnel|pipeline.webp) plus shot-jarvis.webp (focus mode with Jarvis’ briefing, first in the hero), shot-academy.webp (the Academy with a running tour – in German, like its tours), shot-themes.webp (four looks side by side: standard, dark with duotone icons, area colours, a white-label logo) and shot-xr.webp (a board in the 3D preview), converts them to WebP in Chromium (each well under 250 KB), renders the social cards og-en.png and og-de.png (1200×630, the page’s own font, the HyperLevel mark and the tagline) plus og-waitlist-en.png and og-waitlist-de.png for the cloud waitlist mode (“Cloud bald verfügbar”, no cloud price), and refreshes stats.json (--no-shots: only the cards and the stats). Every screenshot is shown only while its file exists, at the width and height read from the file’s header; a picture that fails to load anyway hides its figure (the page’s script), and the spotlight text then takes the full width. Everything lands in web/public/landing/, which the build copies to dist-web/landing/. The font is Inter Tight as a variable font (SIL OFL 1.1, served from the same folder – no font CDN); headings use weight 600, text 400.

Security, speed, accessibility

Section titled “Security, speed, accessibility”
  • Own Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none', images, fonts and the form only from this host, styles inline, and exactly one inline script allowed by its SHA-256 hash. No cookies, X-Frame-Options: DENY.
  • One HTML response plus the font and the images; the hero’s first screen is loaded with priority, the others lazily. Cache-Control: public, max-age=300.
  • The GitHub star count is fetched in the background (at most every 6 hours, 5 s timeout) and never on a visitor’s request; it is left out when GitHub does not answer – or in demo mode, where nothing may leave the server.
  • Semantic HTML (one <main>, skip link, headings in order, labelled form controls, table headers), WCAG AA contrast, works from 360 px wide. tests/landing.test.ts runs the BFSG check of the website builder (services/site-a11y.ts) on the rendered pages and checks the colour pairs, the CSP hash, the shared-link result and that the inline script computes the same numbers as the server.
